A Forward Model for an Active Tactile Sensor Using Echo State Networks

Dr. Nalin Harischandra, Volker Durr · 2012

This paper proposes an Echo State Network (ESN)-based forward model to predict the vibration signal expected at the robotic antenna during movement. The model uses antenna position, robot platform acceleration, and sensor data to estimate self-generated vibrations caused by ego-motion. By comparing the predicted and actual signals, the system successfully separated true contact events from motion-induced disturbances, correctly detecting 34 touch events with 100% accuracy. This ESN-based ego-motion cancellation method forms the core algorithm of our project.

Prediction of Self-induced Mechanoreceptive Sensor Readings in An Insect-inspired Active Tactile Sensing System

Dr.Nalin Harischandra , Volker Durr· 2013

This paper extends the vibration-based tactile sensing system by using two actively moving antennae and an Echo State Network (ESN) to predict self-generated sensor signals. The model combines robot vibration measurements with antenna motion commands to estimate the effects of ego-motion and remove false contact detections. Tested under straight motion, curved motion, and walking-like impacts, the system successfully distinguished 53 true touch events from 252 disturbances with approximately 96% accuracy. This work demonstrates a more advanced version of the sensing platform used in our project.

Distance Estimation And Material Classification of a Compliant Tactile Sensor Using Vibration Modes and Support Vector Machine

Dr. N. Harischandra, Dr. L. Samaranayake, S.R. Gunasekara, H. N. T. K. Kaldera

This study improved contact distance estimation and material recognition using a vibration-based robotic antenna. Vibration signals from an MPU6050 accelerometer mounted on an acrylic antenna were analyzed using FFT, and an SVM classifier was used to identify contact properties. The results showed that the antenna vibration mode changes near 80% of its length, causing non-linear distance measurements close to the tip. Compared with an ANN, the SVM achieved 99.2% classification accuracy with lower computational requirements.

Insect-Inspired Tactile Contour Sampling Using Vibration-Based Robotic Antennae

Thierry Hoinville1, Nalin Harischandra1, Andr´e F. Krause, and Volker D¨urr

This study implemented the Contour-net algorithm on a robot with vibration-based antennae. A Hopf oscillator-based CPG controlled antennal motion, while contact-triggered phase shifts enabled automatic contour following. The system combined tactile sensing and vibration-based distance estimation to localize contacts in 3D space, making it the most relevant physical implementation for our project.

Stable phase-shift despite quasi-rhythmic movements: a CPG-driven dynamic model of active tactile exploration in an insect

Nalin Harischandra, André F. Krause 2 and Volker Dürr · 2015

This research developed a bio-inspired Central Pattern Generator (CPG) model to reproduce the rhythmic antennal movements of stick insects during active tactile exploration. The controller combined phase-coupled Hopf oscillators for rhythm generation with a pattern formation network based on Fourier components to generate realistic joint movements. Simulations showed that maintaining a stable 10–30° phase lead between antennal joints is essential for producing efficient elliptical scanning trajectories, while proprioceptive sensors mainly regulate movement amplitude rather than coordination. This work provides the biological foundation for the oscillatory antenna motion used in our Pan-Tilt sensing system.

Antennal Movements and Mechanoreception: Neurobiology of Active Tactile Sensors

Erich M. Staudachera, Michael Gebhardtby, Volker Du¨rr

This paper explores how insects use antennae and mechanoreceptors for active tactile sensing and object detection. It shows how sensory feedback and antennal movements enable accurate contact localization while reducing the effects of self-motion. The biological principles presented in this work directly inspire the vibration-based robotic antenna and ego-motion cancellation approach used in our project.

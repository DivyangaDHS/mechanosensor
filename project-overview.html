PROJECT OVERVIEW

Bio-Inspired Tactile Sensing for Mobile Robots

This project develops an artificial robotic antenna inspired by insect sensing systems. An accelerometer placed at the tip of a flexible plastic tube detects vibration caused by environmental contact, while a pan-tilt mechanism actively moves the antenna on a mobile robot. The main challenge is separating real contact signals from vibrations created by the robot's own movement.

// FOUNDATION

What Is Tactile Sensing?

Tactile sensing is the ability of a robotic system to detect physical interaction through touch. Instead of relying only on cameras, lidar, or ultrasonic sensors, a tactile sensor measures contact forces, deformation, pressure, vibration, or surface texture when the robot touches an object.

In this project, contact is detected through vibration. When the artificial antenna touches an obstacle, the flexible tube bends and vibrates. The accelerometer at its tip measures this motion as changes along its sensing axes. These signals can then be analysed to identify when and how contact occurred.

Why it matters: tactile sensing remains useful in darkness, smoke, dust, underwater environments, or cluttered areas where visual sensing may become unreliable.

Contact Detection

Recognises the instant at which the antenna touches an object or surface.

Vibration Measurement

Uses the accelerometer response to observe vibration intensity and direction.

Spatial Awareness

Combines antenna motion and contact information to infer where an obstacle is located.

Low-Visibility Operation

Supports perception where cameras cannot provide reliable environmental information.

// HARDWARE

How the Robotic Antenna Works

The sensing system combines a flexible antenna, an inertial sensor, an actuated pan-tilt mechanism, and a mobile robot platform. Each component contributes to either creating movement, measuring vibration, or processing the sensor response.

STEP 01

Active Scanning

The pan-tilt unit sweeps the antenna through a controlled motion to explore the nearby environment.

STEP 02

Vibration Acquisition

The accelerometer records motion caused by servo activity, platform vibration, and external contact.

STEP 03

Ego-Motion Estimation

The algorithm predicts the vibration expected from the robot's own movement.

STEP 04

True Contact Output

The predicted self-generated component is removed, leaving a clearer contact-related signal.

// CORE PROBLEM

The Self-Sensing Problem

A moving robotic antenna does not measure only external contact. The pan-tilt servos, mobile platform, mechanical joints, and structural vibration also affect the accelerometer. As a result, the sensor may interpret the robot's own motion as if it were touching an object.

This unwanted response is called ego-motion-related self-sensing. Without cancellation, the system can produce false contact detections and unreliable localisation results.

// PROPOSED SOLUTION

Ego-Motion Cancellation

The proposed method learns the relationship between commanded robot motion and the vibration measured by the antenna. An Echo State Network, a lightweight recurrent neural network, can model the time-dependent vibration pattern produced by the pan-tilt mechanism and mobile platform.

During operation, the network estimates the self-generated vibration. This prediction is subtracted from the measured signal, allowing the remaining signal to represent actual contact more clearly.

Temporal Learning

Captures how vibration develops over time rather than analysing each sample independently.

Real-Time Potential

Provides a computationally efficient structure suitable for embedded implementation.

// DATA PIPELINE

From Raw Motion to Contact Decision

1. Sensor Sampling

Accelerometer data and actuator commands are sampled at a fixed rate.

2. Preprocessing

Bias removal, scaling, filtering, and synchronisation prepare the signals for learning.

3. ESN Prediction

The model estimates the vibration expected from ego-motion alone.

4. Contact Detection

The residual signal is analysed using thresholds or a classifier to identify genuine contact.

// USE CASES

Potential Applications

A vibration-based tactile antenna can provide an additional sensing channel for robots operating in difficult environments. It can support navigation, obstacle detection, surface exploration, and close-range interaction when vision is limited or unavailable.

Expected Project Outcome

A working mobile robotic platform capable of moving an artificial antenna, measuring vibration, cancelling ego-motion effects, and detecting true environmental contacts more reliably.

The final system will also provide experimental evidence showing the improvement in contact-signal clarity before and after cancellation.
